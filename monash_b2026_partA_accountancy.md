# Monash B2026 — Part A: Accountancy Major Notes

## Overview
The Accountancy major at Monash University Malaysia (B2026) builds foundational knowledge in financial reporting, management accounting, finance, law, economics, statistics, and management. This document consolidates detailed notes based on the learning outcomes for each core unit.

---

## Unit 1: ACW1120 — Financial Accounting 1

### Learning Outcomes

#### LO1: Identify and analyse measurement systems and their relationship with the accounting Conceptual Framework
- **Key Concepts:**
  - The Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting (IFRS-based)
  - Historical cost vs fair value measurement
  - Assumptions: going concern, accrual basis, consistency
  - Qualitative characteristics: relevance and faithful representation (fundamental); comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability (enhancing)
- **Why it matters:** Measurement systems determine how assets, liabilities, income, and expenses are valued and reported. Understanding the framework ensures financial statements are prepared on a theoretically sound basis.

#### LO2: Apply the principles of double-entry and accrual accounting
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Double-entry system: every debit has a corresponding credit
  - Accounting equation: Assets = Liabilities + Equity
  - Accrual basis: transactions recorded when earned/incurred, not when cash moves
  - Journal entries, ledger accounts, trial balance
- **Why it matters:** Double-entry bookkeeping is the foundation of all accounting systems. Accrual accounting provides a more accurate picture of financial performance than cash basis.

#### LO3: Describe and apply definitions and recognition criteria for assets, liabilities, incomes and expenses
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Assets:** Resources controlled by the entity from past events, expected to generate future economic benefits
  - **Liabilities:** Present obligations from past events, expected to result in outflow of resources
  - **Income:** Increases in economic benefits (inflows or enhancements of assets, decreases of liabilities)
  - **Expenses:** Decreases in economic benefits (outflows or depletions of assets, increases of liabilities)
  - Recognition criteria: probable future economic benefit + reliably measurable
- **Why it matters:** Correct classification and recognition ensure financial statements faithfully represent the entity's position and performance.

#### LO4: Apply principles of selected Accounting Standards and prepare financial statements
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Selected IFRS/Accounting Standards applicable to introductory level
  - Preparation of:
    - Statement of Profit or Loss (Income Statement)
    - Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet)
    - Statement of Changes in Equity
  - Focus on service and retail organisations
- **Why it matters:** Practical ability to construct compliant financial statements is a core competency for accountants.

#### LO5: Apply critical thinking, problem solving, and communication skills within corporate financial reporting topics
- **Skills Developed:**
  - Analysing complex business transactions
  - Evaluating alternative accounting treatments
  - Communicating financial information clearly to stakeholders
- **Why it matters:** Technical knowledge alone is insufficient; accountants must interpret and communicate financial data effectively.

## Unit 2: BFW1001 — Foundations of Finance

### Learning Outcomes

#### LO1: Describe fundamental finance concepts applicable to both business and/or personal finance
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Time value of money (present value, future value, discounting, compounding)
  - Risk and return trade-off
  - Interest rates and their determinants
  - Financial markets and institutions
  - Personal finance: budgeting, saving, investing basics
- **Why it matters:** Finance fundamentals underpin all business decisions, from capital budgeting to personal wealth management.

#### LO2: Employ financial software, critical thinking, and problem-solving skills to demonstrate practical understanding
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Spreadsheet modelling (Excel) for financial calculations
  - Using financial calculators or software for NPV, IRR, annuity calculations
  - Analysing financial scenarios and identifying optimal solutions
- **Why it matters:** Modern finance requires computational tools. Practical application bridges theory and real-world decision-making.

#### LO3: Apply the fundamental concepts of finance to real-world financial scenarios using market and financial data
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Reading and interpreting financial market data
  - Applying valuation techniques to real companies/assets
  - Understanding market efficiency and information incorporation
  - Practical investment analysis
- **Why it matters:** Finance is applied, not theoretical. Using real data develops market awareness and analytical capability.

## Unit 3: BTW1042 — Business Law

### Learning Outcomes

#### LO1: Identify the salient features of the Malaysian legal system
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Sources of Malaysian law: Federal Constitution, legislation, delegated legislation, common law, equity, customary law (Adat), Islamic law (Syariah)
  - Law-making institutions: Parliament, State Legislative Assemblies
  - Statutory interpretation: literal, golden, mischief rules
  - Dispute resolution: courts (hierarchy), arbitration, mediation, tribunals
- **Why it matters:** Understanding the legal system is essential for business compliance and risk management.

#### LO2: Apply the principles of contract law, tort law, and agency law to hypothetical legal problems
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Contract Law:** Offer, acceptance, consideration, intention, capacity, consent, legality; terms (express, implied); breach and remedies
  - **Tort Law:** Negligence (duty of care, breach, causation, remoteness, defences); nuisance, defamation basics
  - **Agency Law:** Creation of agency (express, implied, ratification, estoppel); authority (actual, apparent); duties and liabilities of agent/principal
- **Why it matters:** Contracts form the basis of commercial transactions. Tort and agency law govern liability and representation in business.

#### LO3: Examine the legal differences between partnerships and corporations
- **Key Concepts:**
  - **Partnership:** Defined by Partnership Act 1961; unlimited liability (general partners); fiduciary duties; dissolution
  - **Corporation (Company):** Separate legal personality (Salomon principle); limited liability; perpetual succession; formal incorporation under Companies Act 2016
  - **Comparison:** Liability, taxation, management structure, capital raising, regulatory burden, continuity
- **Why it matters:** Choice of business structure affects liability exposure, tax obligations, and operational flexibility.

#### LO4: Demonstrate basic legal research using primary and secondary sources
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Primary sources: statutes, case law, regulations
  - Secondary sources: textbooks, journal articles, legal commentaries
  - Legal databases and research methodology
  - Citation conventions
- **Why it matters:** Legal research skills enable independent analysis of legal issues and informed business decision-making.

#### LO5: Analyse the comparative aspects of Common Law within the context of business law topics
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Common Law tradition inherited from English legal system
  - Doctrine of judicial precedent (stare decisis)
  - Comparison with civil law jurisdictions (brief overview)
  - Adaptation of Common Law in Malaysian context
- **Why it matters:** Malaysia's mixed legal system requires understanding how Common Law interacts with local statutes and customary law.

## Unit 4: ECM1953 — Economics

### Learning Outcomes

#### LO1: Employ the principles of demand and supply to determine equilibrium prices, quantities and profits across various market structures
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Demand: law of demand, determinants, shifts vs movements
  - Supply: law of supply, determinants, shifts vs movements
  - Market equilibrium: price determination, shortages, surpluses
  - Market structures: perfect competition, monopoly, monopolistic competition, oligopoly
  - Profit maximisation: MR = MC rule
- **Why it matters:** Understanding market mechanics is fundamental to business strategy, pricing, and competitive analysis.

#### LO2: Analyse the effects of changes in exogenous factors on prices, quantities, market efficiency, and international trade
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Exogenous shocks: policy changes, technology, consumer preferences, external events
  - Price elasticity of demand and supply
  - Consumer and producer surplus; deadweight loss
  - International trade: comparative advantage, trade barriers, exchange rates
- **Why it matters:** Businesses operate in dynamic environments. Understanding external factor impacts enables proactive strategy adjustment.

#### LO3: Collect and analyse secondary data on macroeconomic variables to examine effects of government policy changes
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Macroeconomic indicators: GDP, inflation (CPI), unemployment, interest rates
  - Fiscal policy: government spending, taxation, budget deficits/surpluses
  - Monetary policy: central bank actions, money supply, interest rate targeting
  - Data sources: World Bank, IMF, Department of Statistics Malaysia, BNM
- **Why it matters:** Macroeconomic conditions directly affect business operations, investment decisions, and forecasting.

#### LO4: Examine the determinants of interest rates and foreign currency exchange rates in the global market
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Interest rate determinants: inflation expectations, monetary policy, risk premium, liquidity preference
  - Exchange rate systems: fixed, floating, managed float
  - Purchasing power parity (PPP) and interest rate parity
  - Factors affecting currency values: trade balances, capital flows, speculation, economic performance
- **Why it matters:** Interest rates affect borrowing costs and investment returns. Exchange rates impact international trade, remittances, and foreign investment.

## Unit 5: ETW1001 — Business Statistics

### Learning Outcomes

#### LO1: Summarise and interpret global business and economic data using appropriate descriptive techniques
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Data types: categorical vs numerical; discrete vs continuous
  - Descriptive statistics: measures of central tendency (mean, median, mode)
  - Measures of dispersion: range, variance, standard deviation, IQR
  - Data visualisation: histograms, box plots, scatter plots, bar charts
  - Frequency distributions and cross-tabulations
- **Why it matters:** Raw data is meaningless without summarisation. Descriptive statistics extract actionable insights from business and economic datasets.

#### LO2: Apply statistical inferential concepts of probability and sampling distributions to solve problems in business and economics
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Probability: rules, conditional probability, independence, Bayes' theorem
  - Probability distributions: normal, binomial, t-distribution
  - Sampling distributions: Central Limit Theorem
  - Confidence intervals for means and proportions
  - Hypothesis testing: null/alternative hypotheses, Type I/II errors, p-values, z-tests, t-tests
- **Why it matters:** Business decisions are made under uncertainty. Inferential statistics allow evidence-based conclusions from sample data.

#### LO3: Assess the relevance and usefulness of predictive modelling to address business and economic challenges
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Correlation vs causation
  - Simple linear regression: least squares method, interpretation of coefficients, R-squared
  - Multiple regression basics
  - Forecasting applications: demand forecasting, sales projections, trend analysis
  - Model limitations and assumptions
- **Why it matters:** Predictive modelling enables data-driven forecasting and strategic planning, reducing reliance on intuition alone.

#### LO4: Communicate statistical results to stakeholders effectively to propose business and economic solutions as a team
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Translating statistical findings into plain language
  - Report writing for non-technical audiences
  - Visual communication of data insights
  - Team collaboration on analytical projects
  - Ethical presentation of data (avoiding misleading statistics)
- **Why it matters:** Statistical analysis has no value if findings cannot be communicated to decision-makers who lack technical training.

## Unit 6: MGW1010 — Introduction to Management

### Learning Outcomes

#### LO1: Discuss fundamental concepts, models and approaches that represent the theory of management
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Historical development: classical (Taylor, Fayol), behavioural (Mayo, McGregor), quantitative, contemporary approaches
  - Management functions: planning, organising, leading, controlling (POLC framework)
  - Management roles (Mintzberg): interpersonal, informational, decisional
  - Management skills: technical, human, conceptual (Katz)
  - Organisational structures and designs
- **Why it matters:** Management theory provides frameworks for understanding how organisations function and how managers create value.

#### LO2: Critically evaluate management theory and research evidence to propose effective business solutions in alignment with responsible management practices
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Critical evaluation of management fads vs evidence-based practices
  - Responsible management: ethics, sustainability, corporate social responsibility (CSR)
  - Stakeholder theory vs shareholder primacy
  - Applying research evidence to practical business problems
  - Decision-making models and biases
- **Why it matters:** Effective managers don't just apply theories uncritically; they evaluate evidence and consider ethical implications of decisions.

#### LO3: Apply communication skills required of the management discipline to address business challenges
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Managerial communication: upward, downward, lateral, external
  - Verbal, written, and non-verbal communication
  - Active listening and feedback
  - Conflict resolution and negotiation
  - Cross-cultural communication in global business
- **Why it matters:** Communication is the primary tool of management. Poor communication is consistently cited as a major cause of organisational failure.

## Unit 7: ACW2220 — Management Accounting

### Learning Outcomes

#### LO1: Classify costs dependent on the context and purpose of related decisions
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Cost classifications:
    - By behaviour: fixed, variable, semi-variable, step-fixed
    - By traceability: direct vs indirect
    - By function: manufacturing (direct materials, direct labour, manufacturing overhead) vs non-manufacturing
    - By relevance: relevant vs irrelevant; sunk costs; opportunity costs
  - Cost objects: products, services, departments, projects, customers
- **Why it matters:** Different decisions require different cost classifications. Misclassification leads to poor decision-making.

#### LO2: Analyse cost behaviour and estimate costs
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Cost behaviour patterns and cost drivers
  - Methods of cost estimation:
    - High-low method
    - Scattergraph/visual fit method
    - Regression analysis (least squares)
  - Cost functions: Y = a + bX
  - Relevant range concept
- **Why it matters:** Understanding how costs behave with activity levels enables accurate budgeting, pricing, and forecasting.

#### LO3: Design, use and evaluate costing systems
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Job costing vs process costing vs activity-based costing (ABC)
  - Overhead allocation: traditional (plant-wide, departmental rates) vs ABC (multiple cost pools and drivers)
  - Costing system selection criteria: nature of production, information needs, cost-benefit trade-off
  - Evaluation: accuracy, timeliness, behavioural impacts
- **Why it matters:** Costing systems determine product/service costs, which directly affect pricing, profitability analysis, and inventory valuation.

#### LO4: Analyse and use cost information relevant to decision-making
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Short-term decision-making scenarios:
    - Special order decisions
    - Make-or-buy (outsourcing) decisions
    - Product mix decisions with constraints
    - Sell-or-process-further decisions
    - Add-or-drop product line/segment decisions
  - Relevant cost analysis: focus on future, differential costs
- **Why it matters:** Management accountants provide decision-relevant information. Understanding what costs matter for specific decisions is a core skill.

#### LO5: Communicate cost information effectively to support managerial planning, control, and decision-making
- **Key Concepts:**
  - Budgeting: master budget components (operating, financial, capital)
  - Variance analysis: flexible budgets, price and quantity variances
  - Performance reporting: responsibility accounting, controllable vs non-controllable costs
  - Communicating variances and recommendations to management
  - Behavioural aspects of budgeting (budgetary slack, participation, motivation)
- **Why it matters:** Management accounting is a service function. Cost information must be presented clearly and actionably to influence managerial behaviour positively.

## Integration: How These Units Fit Together in Accountancy

| Unit | Domain | Role in Accountancy |
|------|--------|---------------------|
| ACW1120 | Financial Accounting | External reporting foundation — how accountants communicate with investors, regulators, and the public |
| BFW1001 | Finance | Capital allocation, investment appraisal, and understanding the financial environment |
| BTW1042 | Business Law | Legal compliance, contract management, and understanding business structures |
| ECM1953 | Economics | Understanding market forces, macroeconomic policy impacts, and global economic context |
| ETW1001 | Statistics | Data-driven decision making, risk analysis, and empirical research skills |
| MGW1010 | Management | Organisational context, leadership, and responsible management practice |
| ACW2220 | Management Accounting | Internal decision support — planning, control, and strategic cost management |

### Key Themes Across the Major

1. **Financial Reporting Chain:** ACW1120 (foundations) → advanced financial accounting → external reporting
2. **Decision Support Chain:** ACW2220 (cost basics) → advanced management accounting → strategic business partnering
3. **Analytical Foundation:** ETW1001 (statistics) + ECM1953 (economics) → quantitative analysis in accounting and finance
4. **Business Context:** BTW1042 (legal) + MGW1010 (management) + BFW1001 (finance) → holistic understanding of organisational environment

## Study Tips for Accountancy Major

- **Build the double-entry foundation early** — ACW1120 concepts recur throughout the degree
- **Link accounting and finance** — BFW1001 time value concepts connect to ACW2220 capital budgeting
- **Practise legal reasoning** — BTW1042 requires case analysis, not just memorisation
- **Master Excel/spreadsheet skills** — Essential for ETW1001, BFW1001, and ACW2220
- **Think about the "why"** — Management accounting especially requires understanding decision context, not just calculations
- **Stay current** — Follow Malaysian business news (The Edge, Bursa Malaysia announcements) to see accounting concepts in practice
