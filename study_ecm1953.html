Multiple Choice Questions

Q1: Which of the following best describes the concept of opportunity cost?
  • A) The total monetary cost of producing a good
  • B) The value of the next best alternative forgone when making a decision
  • C) The sunk costs incurred in past production decisions
  • D) The difference between total revenue and total cost
Show Answer
Answer: B
Opportunity cost is defined as the value of the next best alternative that must be given up when a choice is made. It is a fundamental concept in economics that applies to all decision-making under scarcity.
Q2: If the price elasticity of demand for a product is -0.5, which statement is correct?
  • A) Demand is elastic and a price increase will reduce total revenue
  • B) Demand is inelastic and a price increase will increase total revenue
  • C) Demand is unit elastic and total revenue remains unchanged
  • D) Demand is perfectly inelastic and quantity demanded does not change
Show Answer
Answer: B
When |PED| < 1, demand is inelastic. With inelastic demand, the percentage change in quantity demanded is smaller than the percentage change in price, so a price increase raises total revenue.
Q3: In a perfectly competitive market, a firm's short-run supply curve is:
  • A) The portion of its AVC curve above the MC curve
  • B) The portion of its MC curve above the minimum AVC
  • C) The entire MC curve
  • D) The portion of its ATC curve above the MC curve
Show Answer
Answer: B
In perfect competition, a firm will produce in the short run only if price covers average variable cost. Therefore, the supply curve is the marginal cost (MC) curve above the minimum point of the average variable cost (AVC) curve.
Q4: Which policy combination would be most appropriate to address demand-pull inflation?
  • A) Expansionary fiscal policy and expansionary monetary policy
  • B) Contractionary fiscal policy and contractionary monetary policy
  • C) Expansionary fiscal policy and contractionary monetary policy
  • D) Contractionary fiscal policy and expansionary monetary policy
Show Answer
Answer: B
Demand-pull inflation occurs when aggregate demand exceeds aggregate supply. Contractionary (tight) fiscal policy (reduced government spending or higher taxes) and contractionary monetary policy (higher interest rates or reduced money supply) both reduce aggregate demand.
Q5: The Lorenz curve illustrates:
  • A) The relationship between inflation and unemployment
  • B) The distribution of income or wealth within a population
  • C) The trade-off between current and future consumption
  • D) The production possibilities frontier of an economy
Show Answer
Answer: B
The Lorenz curve graphs the cumulative share of income (or wealth) against the cumulative share of the population. Greater curvature away from the line of perfect equality indicates higher inequality.
Q6: A monopolist maximises profit by producing at the quantity where:
  • A) Price equals marginal cost
  • B) Marginal revenue equals marginal cost
  • C) Average revenue equals average cost
  • D) Marginal revenue equals average cost
Show Answer
Answer: B
All profit-maximising firms produce where marginal revenue (MR) equals marginal cost (MC). For a monopolist, MR lies below the demand curve because it must lower the price on all units to sell an additional unit.
Q7: Which of the following would cause a leftward shift in the aggregate demand curve?
  • A) An increase in consumer confidence
  • B) A decrease in government spending
  • C) A depreciation of the domestic currency
  • D) A cut in personal income tax rates
Show Answer
Answer: B
A decrease in government spending directly reduces one component of aggregate demand (AD = C + I + G + NX), shifting the AD curve to the left. The other options would shift AD to the right.
Q8: According to the Phillips curve, in the short run:
  • A) There is no trade-off between inflation and unemployment
  • B) Lower unemployment is associated with lower inflation
  • C) Lower unemployment is associated with higher inflation
  • D) Inflation and unemployment always move in the same direction
Show Answer
Answer: C
The short-run Phillips curve shows an inverse relationship: as unemployment falls below the natural rate, inflation tends to rise due to increased wage and price pressures. In the long run, the curve is vertical at the natural rate of unemployment.
Q9: Which of the following is a characteristic of a public good?
  • A) Excludability and rivalry in consumption
  • B) Non-excludability and non-rivalry in consumption
  • C) Excludability but non-rivalry in consumption
  • D) Rivalry in consumption but non-excludability
Show Answer
Answer: B
Public goods are defined by non-excludability (it is costly or impossible to prevent someone from using the good) and non-rivalry (one person's consumption does not diminish another's). Examples include national defence and street lighting.
Q10: When a country has a comparative advantage in producing a good, it means that:
  • A) It can produce the good using fewer resources than any other country
  • B) It has a lower opportunity cost of producing that good relative to other countries
  • C) It has an absolute advantage in producing all goods
  • D) Its production possibilities frontier lies outside those of other countries
Show Answer
Answer: B
Comparative advantage is determined by lower opportunity cost, not absolute resource use. A country should specialise in goods where it has the lowest relative opportunity cost and trade for other goods.

Subjective Questions

Q: Explain the law of diminishing marginal returns and illustrate with a numerical example.Production Theory
Answer
The law of diminishing marginal returns states that as successive units of a variable input (e.g., labour) are added to fixed inputs (e.g., capital and land), there is a point beyond which the additional output (marginal product) from each extra unit of the variable input begins to decline.

Example: Consider a factory with 5 machines (fixed).
• 1 worker produces 10 units/day (MP = 10)
• 2 workers produce 25 units/day (MP = 15)
• 3 workers produce 36 units/day (MP = 11)
• 4 workers produce 40 units/day (MP = 4)
• 5 workers produce 42 units/day (MP = 2)

After the 2nd worker, each additional worker contributes less to total output than the previous one. This occurs because fixed inputs become overcrowded or overused. The law is central to understanding short-run cost curves and explains why marginal cost eventually rises.
Q: Distinguish between GDP and GNP. Which measure is more commonly used for domestic policy analysis and why?Macroeconomic Measurement
Answer
GDP (Gross Domestic Product) measures the total value of all final goods and services produced within a country's geographical boundaries during a specific period, regardless of who owns the factors of production.

GNP (Gross National Product), now more commonly called GNI (Gross National Income), measures the total income earned by a country's residents and businesses, including income from abroad, regardless of where the production occurs.

Formula: GNP = GDP + Net factor income from abroad (income earned by residents overseas minus income earned by foreigners domestically).

GDP is more commonly used for domestic policy analysis because it directly reflects economic activity occurring within the country, making it more relevant for assessing domestic employment, tax bases, and infrastructure needs. It is also easier to measure accurately using domestic data collection systems.
Q: Using a diagram, explain how a price ceiling set below the equilibrium price affects market outcomes. Discuss one real-world example.Market Intervention
Answer
A price ceiling is a legal maximum price set by the government. When set below the equilibrium price (a binding price ceiling), it creates a shortage because quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at that price.

Diagram explanation:
• Draw standard supply (upward sloping) and demand (downward sloping) curves intersecting at equilibrium price P* and quantity Q*.
• Draw a horizontal price ceiling line at Pc below P*.
• At Pc, quantity demanded (Qd) is read off the demand curve; quantity supplied (Qs) is read off the supply curve.
• Since Qd > Qs, a shortage of (Qd - Qs) occurs.

Consequences include:
1. Shortages and queuing
2. Black markets where goods trade above the legal maximum
3. Reduced quality as producers cut costs
4. Non-price rationing mechanisms (e.g., waiting lists, favouritism)

Real-world example: Rent control in cities like New York or Melbourne (historically) keeps rental prices below market rates, leading to housing shortages, reduced maintenance incentives for landlords, and long waiting lists for affordable housing.
Q: Calculate and interpret the consumer surplus and producer surplus given the demand function P = 50 - 2Q and supply function P = 10 + 2Q.Welfare Economics
Answer
Step 1: Find equilibrium by setting demand equal to supply.
50 - 2Q = 10 + 2Q
40 = 4Q
Q* = 10
P* = 50 - 2(10) = 30

Step 2: Find choke prices.
Demand choke price (Q=0): P = 50
Supply choke price (Q=0): P = 10

Step 3: Calculate areas.
Consumer Surplus (CS) = ½ × base × height = ½ × 10 × (50 - 30) = ½ × 10 × 20 = 100
Producer Surplus (PS) = ½ × base × height = ½ × 10 × (30 - 10) = ½ × 10 × 20 = 100
Total Surplus = CS + PS = 200

Interpretation: Consumer surplus of 100 represents the total benefit consumers receive by paying 30 instead of the maximum they would have been willing to pay (up to 50). Producer surplus of 100 represents the benefit producers receive by selling at 30 instead of the minimum they would have accepted (as low as 10). In this symmetric linear case, surplus is split equally between consumers and producers.
Q: Explain the functions of money and discuss why cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin may struggle to fulfil one or more of these functions effectively.Monetary Economics
Answer
Money serves three primary functions:

1. Medium of exchange: Money eliminates the need for barter by acting as an intermediary accepted in transactions. It reduces transaction costs and the problem of double coincidence of wants.

2. Unit of account: Money provides a common measure of value, allowing prices to be quoted and economic value to be compared. It simplifies accounting and economic calculation.

3. Store of value: Money allows purchasing power to be transferred from the present to the future. It retains value over time so people can save and plan.

Why Bitcoin struggles:
• Medium of exchange: Limited merchant acceptance, high transaction fees during network congestion, and slow confirmation times make it impractical for everyday purchases compared to fiat currency or credit cards.
• Unit of account: Extreme price volatility means prices denominated in Bitcoin fluctuate wildly, making it unreliable for long-term contracts or price setting. Most goods are still priced in national currencies.
• Store of value: While some view Bitcoin as "digital gold," its volatility makes it risky as a store of value. A 20-30% price swing in a week is common, unlike stable fiat currencies which typically fluctuate by small percentages annually.

These limitations explain why central banks continue to focus on fiat currency and are exploring more stable central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) rather than adopting decentralised cryptocurrencies as primary money.
