Multiple Choice Questions

Q1: Which of the following is a characteristic of direct costs but NOT indirect costs?
  • A) They cannot be traced to a specific cost object in an economically feasible way
  • B) They can be conveniently and economically traced to a specific cost object
  • C) They are always fixed regardless of output level
  • D) They are excluded from product costing under absorption costing
Show Answer
Answer: B
Direct costs are those that can be conveniently and economically traced to a specific cost object (e.g., a product, department, or job). Indirect costs, by contrast, cannot be traced in an economically feasible way and must be allocated using a cost allocation base.
Q2: Under absorption costing, fixed manufacturing overheads are treated as:
  • A) period costs and expensed in the current period
  • B) product costs and included in the cost of inventory
  • C) variable costs that change with production volume
  • D) administrative expenses excluded from gross profit
Show Answer
Answer: B
Under absorption (full) costing, all manufacturing costs — both variable and fixed — are treated as product costs. Fixed manufacturing overheads are allocated to units produced and become part of the cost of inventory. They are only expensed as cost of goods sold when the inventory is sold.
Q3: A company has budgeted production of 10,000 units and fixed overhead of $100,000. The predetermined fixed overhead rate per unit is $10. If actual production is 11,000 units and actual fixed overhead is $105,000, the fixed overhead volume variance is:
  • A) $10,000 favourable
  • B) $10,000 unfavourable
  • C) $5,000 favourable
  • D) $5,000 unfavourable
Show Answer
Answer: A
Fixed overhead volume variance = (Actual production − Budgeted production) × Fixed overhead rate = (11,000 − 10,000) × $10 = $10,000. Because actual production exceeded budgeted production, more overhead was absorbed than budgeted, resulting in a favourable volume variance.
Q4: In a make-or-buy decision, which of the following costs is generally irrelevant?
  • A) The purchase price of the component from an external supplier
  • B) The avoidable fixed costs if production ceases
  • C) The unavoidable allocated fixed factory overhead that will continue regardless
  • D) The direct materials cost saved by not making the component
Show Answer
Answer: C
Unavoidable allocated fixed overheads are sunk or committed costs that will be incurred regardless of the make-or-buy decision. They are therefore irrelevant to the decision. Only costs and benefits that differ between the alternatives (differential costs) are relevant.
Q5: Which budgeting approach requires managers to justify all expenses for each new period, starting from a "zero base"?
  • A) Incremental budgeting
  • B) Zero-based budgeting (ZBB)
  • C) Rolling (continuous) budgeting
  • D) Activity-based budgeting
Show Answer
Answer: B
Zero-based budgeting (ZBB) requires all expenses to be justified from scratch for each new budget period. Unlike incremental budgeting, which starts with the prior period's budget and adjusts it, ZBB evaluates every activity and its costs anew, promoting cost efficiency and eliminating obsolete expenditures.
Q6: The contribution margin ratio is calculated as:
  • A) Fixed costs divided by sales revenue
  • B) Contribution margin divided by sales revenue
  • C) Operating profit divided by sales revenue
  • D) Variable costs divided by sales revenue
Show Answer
Answer: B
Contribution margin ratio = Contribution margin ÷ Sales revenue. Alternatively, it can be calculated as (Sales revenue − Variable costs) ÷ Sales revenue, or 1 − Variable cost ratio. It indicates what percentage of each sales dollar is available to cover fixed costs and generate profit.
Q7: In Activity-Based Costing (ABC), a "cost driver" is defined as:
  • A) the total cost of a specific activity pool
  • B) a factor that causes or influences the cost of an activity
  • C) the difference between actual and budgeted activity costs
  • D) a method of allocating direct labour hours to products
Show Answer
Answer: B
A cost driver is any factor that has a cause-and-effect relationship with the cost of an activity. Examples include the number of machine setups, inspection hours, or purchase orders. ABC uses cost drivers to allocate overhead costs more accurately than traditional volume-based allocation bases.
Q8: Break-even point in units is calculated as:
  • A) Contribution margin per unit divided by fixed costs
  • B) Fixed costs divided by contribution margin per unit
  • C) Sales revenue divided by variable cost per unit
  • D) Operating profit divided by sales price per unit
Show Answer
Answer: B
Break-even point (units) = Fixed costs ÷ Contribution margin per unit. At this level of sales, total contribution margin exactly equals total fixed costs, resulting in zero operating profit. Any sales above this point generate profit.
Q9: A favourable direct materials price variance indicates that:
  • A) more material was used than the standard allowed
  • B) the actual price paid for materials was less than the standard price
  • C) the actual price paid for materials was more than the standard price
  • D) less material was used than the standard allowed
Show Answer
Answer: B
Direct materials price variance = (Actual price − Standard price) × Actual quantity purchased. A favourable variance means the actual price was lower than the standard price, reducing costs relative to the budget. Option D describes a favourable quantity (efficiency) variance, not a price variance.
Q10: Which performance measure is most appropriate for evaluating an investment centre manager?
  • A) Cost variance analysis
  • B) Residual Income (RI) or Return on Investment (ROI)
  • C) Labour efficiency variance only
  • D) Customer satisfaction score alone
Show Answer
Answer: B
An investment centre manager has authority over revenues, costs, and investment decisions. Residual Income (RI) and Return on Investment (ROI) are both comprehensive measures that incorporate profit and the assets used to generate that profit. Cost variance analysis and labour efficiency variance are more suitable for cost centres, while customer satisfaction is a non-financial measure that complements but does not replace financial evaluation.

Subjective Questions

Q1: Distinguish between job costing and process costing, and provide two examples of industries where each method would be most appropriate.Costing Systems
Answer
Job costing is used when products or services are distinct, customised, and produced in identifiable batches or jobs. Costs are accumulated separately for each job, allowing precise tracking of direct materials, direct labour, and allocated overhead per unit of output. Examples: construction companies (each building is unique) and accounting firms (each audit engagement differs).

Process costing is used when homogeneous products are mass-produced in a continuous flow through standardised processes. Costs are accumulated by department or process for a period and then averaged over all units produced. Examples: petrochemical refineries (continuous production of standardised fuel grades) and food manufacturers (mass production of identical packaged goods).
Q2: A company sells a single product for $80 per unit. Variable costs are $50 per unit, and total fixed costs are $90,000 per month. Calculate: (a) the break-even point in units and dollars; (b) the number of units required to earn a target profit of $30,000; (c) the margin of safety if actual sales are 4,500 units.Cost-Volume-Profit Analysis
Answer
Contribution margin per unit = $80 − $50 = $30
Contribution margin ratio = $30 ÷ $80 = 37.5%

(a) Break-even point:
  Units = Fixed costs ÷ CM per unit = $90,000 ÷ $30 = 3,000 units
  Dollars = Fixed costs ÷ CM ratio = $90,000 ÷ 0.375 = $240,000

(b) Target profit of $30,000:
  Units = (Fixed costs + Target profit) ÷ CM per unit = ($90,000 + $30,000) ÷ $30 = 4,000 units

(c) Margin of safety:
  MOS (units) = Actual sales − Break-even sales = 4,500 − 3,000 = 1,500 units
  MOS (%) = 1,500 ÷ 4,500 = 33.3%
Q3: Explain why absorption costing and variable costing may produce different profit figures for the same period, and identify the condition under which both methods report the same profit.Absorption vs Variable Costing
Answer
The difference arises from the treatment of fixed manufacturing overhead. Under absorption costing, fixed manufacturing overhead is treated as a product cost and included in inventory; it is expensed only when the inventory is sold. Under variable costing, fixed manufacturing overhead is treated as a period cost and expensed in full in the period incurred.

When production exceeds sales, absorption costing defers some fixed overhead in ending inventory, resulting in higher profit than variable costing. When sales exceed production (inventory decreases), previously deferred fixed overhead is released, causing absorption costing profit to be lower.

Both methods report the same profit when production equals sales (i.e., there is no change in inventory levels over the period). In this case, no fixed overhead is deferred or released from inventory.
Q4: Describe the key steps in preparing a master budget, and explain the role of the sales budget as the starting point.Budgeting
Answer
The master budget is a comprehensive plan integrating all functional budgets and culminating in budgeted financial statements. Key steps include:

1. Sales budget — forecasts unit sales and revenue for the period.
2. Production budget — determines units to produce based on sales and inventory policy.
3. Direct materials, direct labour, and manufacturing overhead budgets — quantify resources needed for production.
4. Ending inventory budget — plans for finished goods and raw materials inventory.
5. Cost of goods sold budget — synthesises production cost information.
6. Selling and administrative expense budget — plans non-manufacturing expenditures.
7. Cash budget — projects cash receipts and payments.
8. Budgeted financial statements — income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement.

The sales budget is the foundation because sales volume drives production requirements, resource needs, and ultimately cash flows. Inaccurate sales forecasts cascade through the entire master budget, making it the most critical and sensitive component.
Q5: A division of Monash Manufacturing has operating profit of $480,000 and average operating assets of $3,000,000. The company's minimum required rate of return is 12%. Calculate: (a) Return on Investment (ROI); (b) Residual Income (RI); and (c) discuss which measure provides a stronger incentive for the division manager to accept a new investment opportunity earning $45,000 on $400,000 of assets.Performance Measurement / Investment Centres
Answer
(a) Return on Investment (ROI):
  ROI = Operating profit ÷ Average operating assets = $480,000 ÷ $3,000,000 = 16%

(b) Residual Income (RI):
  RI = Operating profit − (Average operating assets × Minimum required rate of return)
  RI = $480,000 − ($3,000,000 × 12%) = $480,000 − $360,000 = $120,000

(c) Evaluation of the new investment:
The new investment yields $45,000 ÷ $400,000 = 11.25%, which is below the current divisional ROI of 16%. A manager evaluated on ROI might reject this investment because it would dilute the division's overall ROI, even though the investment exceeds the company's minimum required return of 12%.

Under Residual Income, the new investment would increase RI because its return (11.25%) exceeds the minimum required return (12%) — wait, 11.25% is actually below 12%. Let me recalculate: the new investment returns $45,000 on $400,000 = 11.25%, which is below the 12% hurdle rate. Therefore, RI would decrease by ($400,000 × 12%) − $45,000 = $3,000. In this specific case, both measures would suggest rejection.

However, conceptually, RI is generally superior because it aligns divisional decisions with corporate goals by using a consistent hurdle rate, avoiding the sub-optimisation problem inherent in ROI where managers reject profitable projects that are below their current average ROI but above the cost of capital.
