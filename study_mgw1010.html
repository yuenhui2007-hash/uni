MGW1010 — Introduction to Management

Multiple Choice Questions

Q1: Which of the following is a primary function of management according to Henri Fayol?
  • A) Marketing and sales
  • B) Planning, organising, leading, and controlling
  • C) Research and development
  • D) Accounting and auditing
Show Answer
Answer: B
Fayol identified five primary functions: planning, organising, commanding, coordinating, and controlling. Modern texts often combine these into planning, organising, leading, and controlling (POLC).
Q2: Under McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y, which statement best describes a Theory X manager's assumption about employees?
  • A) Employees are self-motivated and seek responsibility
  • B) Employees inherently dislike work and must be coerced to perform
  • C) Employees are motivated primarily by social needs
  • D) Employees seek self-actualisation above all else
Show Answer
Answer: B
Theory X assumes that people inherently dislike work, are lazy, and must be controlled, directed, or threatened with punishment to achieve organisational goals.
Q3: In Porter's Five Forces model, the threat of new entrants is typically LOW when:
  • A) Capital requirements are low and switching costs are minimal
  • B) Economies of scale are significant and brand loyalty is high
  • C) Products are undifferentiated and exit barriers are low
  • D) Suppliers are fragmented and buyer concentration is high
Show Answer
Answer: B
High economies of scale and strong brand loyalty create barriers to entry, making it difficult for new competitors to enter the market successfully.
Q4: Which organisational structure groups employees based on the functions they perform (e.g., marketing, finance, operations)?
  • A) Divisional structure
  • B) Matrix structure
  • C) Functional structure
  • D) Network structure
Show Answer
Answer: C
A functional structure groups activities and employees by specialised functions such as marketing, finance, operations, and human resources.
Q5: Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory distinguishes between motivators and hygiene factors. Which of the following is a HYGIENE factor?
  • A) Achievement
  • B) Recognition
  • C) Company policy and administration
  • D) The work itself
Show Answer
Answer: C
Hygiene factors (e.g., salary, working conditions, company policy, supervision) prevent dissatisfaction but do not motivate. Motivators include achievement, recognition, and the work itself.
Q6: According to the contingency approach to management, the most effective management style depends on:
  • A) Universal principles that apply to all organisations
  • B) The specific situation and context in which management occurs
  • C) The size of the organisation alone
  • D) The cultural background of the CEO
Show Answer
Answer: B
The contingency approach argues there is no single best way to manage; the optimal approach depends on situational factors such as task complexity, technology, and external environment.
Q7: In the context of decision-making, what is "bounded rationality"?
  • A) Managers making perfectly rational decisions with complete information
  • B) The idea that managers make decisions within the limits of their cognitive ability and available information
  • C) A decision-making process used exclusively in non-profit organisations
  • D) The principle that all decisions should be made by consensus
Show Answer
Answer: B
Herbert Simon proposed bounded rationality: decision-makers are limited by cognitive constraints, time, and imperfect information, so they "satisfice" rather than optimise.
Q8: Which of the following best describes transformational leadership?
  • A) Leaders who focus on clarifying roles and providing rewards for task completion
  • B) Leaders who inspire and motivate followers to achieve extraordinary outcomes and develop their own leadership capacity
  • C) Leaders who avoid making decisions and allow group consensus to emerge
  • D) Leaders who exercise strict control over employees through rules and procedures
Show Answer
Answer: B
Transformational leaders inspire followers through vision, intellectual stimulation, individualised consideration, and inspirational motivation, fostering higher levels of engagement and performance.
Q9: SWOT analysis is used to evaluate which of the following?
  • A) An organisation's strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats
  • B) An organisation's sales, wages, output, and turnover

SWOT stands for Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats. It is a strategic planning tool used to assess internal capabilities and external conditions.
Q10: In Hofstede's cultural dimensions theory, "uncertainty avoidance" refers to:
  • A) The extent to which members of a society feel uncomfortable with ambiguity and prefer structured situations
  • B) The degree to which less powerful members accept unequal power distribution
  • C) The preference for individual achievement over group harmony
  • D) The emphasis on competition and assertiveness in a society

Answer: A
Uncertainty avoidance measures a society's tolerance for ambiguity. High uncertainty avoidance cultures prefer clear rules, formal structures, and standardised procedures.

Subjective Questions

Q1: Explain Mintzberg's managerial roles and provide an example of each category.Managerial Roles
Answer
Mintzberg identified ten managerial roles grouped into three categories: Interpersonal (figurehead, leader, liaison), Informational (monitor, disseminator, spokesperson), and Decisional (entrepreneur, disturbance handler, resource allocator, negotiator). For example, a manager acting as a figurehead performs ceremonial duties such as greeting visiting dignitaries; as a monitor, they scan the environment for information relevant to the organisation; as a resource allocator, they decide how to distribute budget, equipment, and personnel across projects.
Q2: Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of a matrix organisational structure.Organisational Design
Answer
Advantages: Improved coordination across functional and project lines; flexible use of specialised staff; enhanced information flow; better response to dynamic environments; development of broader managerial skills. Disadvantages: Dual reporting lines can create confusion and conflict; potential for power struggles between functional and project managers; slower decision-making due to need for consensus; increased administrative overhead; risk of employees experiencing role ambiguity and stress from conflicting demands.
Q3: Compare and contrast Maslow's Hierarchy of Needs and Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory as motivation theories.Motivation Theory
Answer
Maslow's Hierarchy proposes five levels of human needs (physiological, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation) arranged hierarchically; once a lower need is satisfied, the next level becomes the primary motivator. Herzberg's Two-Factor Theory classifies factors into motivators (achievement, recognition, work itself, responsibility, advancement) that create satisfaction, and hygiene factors (salary, supervision, policy, working conditions) that prevent dissatisfaction but do not motivate. Key differences: Maslow is need-based and general; Herzberg is job-specific and splits factors into two dimensions. Maslow assumes a sequential progression; Herzberg argues satisfaction and dissatisfaction are independent continua.
Q4: Describe the steps in the strategic management process and explain why environmental scanning is critical.Strategic Management
Answer
The strategic management process typically involves: (1) Establishing mission, vision, and objectives; (2) Environmental scanning (internal and external analysis using tools like SWOT, PESTEL, and Porter's Five Forces); (3) Strategy formulation (corporate, business, and functional strategies); (4) Strategy implementation (allocating resources, structuring the organisation, managing change); and (5) Evaluation and control (measuring performance and taking corrective action). Environmental scanning is critical because it allows managers to identify opportunities and threats in the external environment and align organisational strengths and weaknesses with market conditions, reducing strategic surprise and improving competitive positioning.
Q5: Analyse how globalisation has impacted management practices in Australian organisations, with reference to cultural diversity and ethical considerations.Global & Ethical Management
Answer
Globalisation has compelled Australian managers to adopt more culturally sensitive and adaptive practices. Workforce diversity requires inclusive leadership, cross-cultural communication training, and flexible HR policies. Ethical considerations have intensified due to global supply chains (e.g., labour standards, environmental compliance) and increased scrutiny from stakeholders. Australian organisations must navigate differing legal frameworks, cultural norms, and stakeholder expectations across markets. Managers face challenges such as balancing global integration with local responsiveness, ensuring ethical sourcing, and managing virtual teams across time zones. Successful global managers develop cultural intelligence (CQ), uphold corporate social responsibility (CSR), and implement codes of conduct that transcend national boundaries while respecting local customs.
