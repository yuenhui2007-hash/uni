💡 Key Point

The accounting cycle is a continuous process. Closing entries reset temporary accounts (revenues, expenses, dividends) to zero for the next period, while permanent accounts (assets, liabilities, equity) carry forward.

The Accounting Cycle
1

Identify & Analyse Transactions

Events that affect the entity's financial position. Source documents (invoices, receipts) provide evidence.

2

Record in Journal (Journalising)

Chronological record of transactions. Debit one or more accounts, credit one or more accounts. Total debits = total credits.

3

Post to Ledger

Transfer journal entries to individual accounts in the general ledger. Update T-account balances.

4

Prepare Unadjusted Trial Balance

List all accounts and their balances. Verify that total debits = total credits. Catches recording errors.

5

Adjusting Entries

Accruals (revenues earned/expenses incurred, not yet recorded) and deferrals (prepayments, unearned revenue). Estimates (depreciation, bad debts).

6

Adjusted Trial Balance

Verify debits = credits after adjustments. Used to prepare financial statements.

7

Financial Statements

Income Statement → Statement of Changes in Equity → Balance Sheet → Statement of Cash Flows → Notes.

8

Closing Entries

Reset temporary accounts (revenue, expense, dividend) to zero. Transfer net income/loss to retained earnings.

9

Post-Closing Trial Balance

Verify only permanent accounts remain. Debits = Credits. Ready for next period.

Step 1: Contract

Must have approval, rights identified, payment terms, commercial substance, and collectability is probable.

Step 2: Performance Obligations

A promise to transfer a distinct good/service. Bundle if not distinct. Example: phone + service plan = 2 POs.

Step 3: Transaction Price

Consideration expected. Adjust for: variable consideration, significant financing, non-cash consideration, consideration payable.

Step 4: Allocate Price

Based on standalone selling prices. Use adjusted market assessment, expected cost plus margin, or residual approach.

Step 5: Recognise Revenue

Over time (if criteria met: customer simultaneously receives/consumes, creates asset with no alternative use) or at a point in time.

Key Principle

Recognise revenue to depict the transfer of promised goods/services in an amount reflecting consideration entitled.

Capital Budgeting Process
1

Identify Investment Opportunities

Strategic planning, market research, operational needs. Generate ideas from all levels of the organisation.

2

Estimate Cash Flows

Initial outlay, operating cash flows, terminal value. Ignore sunk costs. Include opportunity costs and externalities. Use after-tax cash flows.

3

Assess Risk

Standalone risk (sensitivity analysis, scenario analysis, simulation). Market risk (beta for CAPM). Adjust discount rate or cash flows.

4

Evaluate using Decision Criteria

NPV (accept if >0), IRR (accept if > WACC), Payback Period, Profitability Index, Accounting Rate of Return. Prefer NPV for mutually exclusive projects.

5

Make the Decision

Board/investment committee approval. Consider strategic fit, capital constraints, risk appetite, and non-financial factors.

6

Implement & Monitor

Project execution, track actual vs budgeted cash flows. Post-audit review to improve future estimates and decision-making.

⚠️ Important Note on NPV vs IRR Conflict

For mutually exclusive projects, NPV and IRR may give different rankings due to differing scale or cash flow timing. Always trust NPV — it directly measures value added to shareholders.
