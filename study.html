Q1: A company receives $12,000 cash for services to be performed next month. What is the correct entry?
  • A) Dr Cash $12,000; Cr Revenue $12,000
  • B) Dr Cash $12,000; Cr Unearned Revenue $12,000
  • C) Dr Revenue $12,000; Cr Cash $12,000
  • D) Dr Prepaid Revenue $12,000; Cr Cash $12,000
Show Answer
Answer: B
Cash received before earning = liability (Unearned Revenue). Revenue recognition principle — recognise when earned, not when cash received.
Q2: Which qualitative characteristic requires information to be complete, neutral, and free from error?
  • A) Relevance
  • B) Comparability
  • C) Faithful Representation
  • D) Timeliness
Show Answer
Answer: C
Faithful representation = complete, neutral, and free from error. It must reflect economic substance, not merely legal form.
Q3: On 1 June, XYZ Ltd pays $24,000 for a 12-month insurance policy. What is the adjusting entry on 30 June?
  • A) Dr Insurance Expense $24,000; Cr Prepaid Insurance $24,000
  • B) Dr Insurance Expense $2,000; Cr Prepaid Insurance $2,000
  • C) Dr Prepaid Insurance $2,000; Cr Insurance Expense $2,000
  • D) No entry needed
Show Answer
Answer: B
$24,000 ÷ 12 months = $2,000 per month. One month has expired, so recognise $2,000 as expense. The remaining $22,000 stays as Prepaid Insurance (asset).

BFW1001 — Foundations of Finance

Time Value of Money Calculators Allowed
Future Value (Lump Sum)
FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ
Present Value (Lump Sum)
PV = FV / (1 + r)ⁿ
Future Value of Annuity
FVA = PMT × [(1 + r)ⁿ - 1] / r
Present Value of Annuity
PVA = PMT × [1 - (1 + r)⁻ⁿ] / r
⚠ Exam Trap

Always check if payments are at beginning (annuity due) or end (ordinary annuity) of period. Annuity due = multiply ordinary annuity result by (1+r).

NPV & IRR Investment Appraisal
Net Present Value
NPV = Σ [CFₜ / (1 + r)ᵗ] - Initial Investment
Decision Rule
NPV > 0 → Accept; NPV < 0 → Reject
IRR
Discount rate where NPV = 0. If IRR > required return → Accept.
Risk & Return Conceptual
Systematic Risk Market-wide, non-diversifiable. Measured by beta (β).
Unsystematic Risk Firm-specific, diversifiable. Reduced via portfolio diversification.
Required Return (CAPM) E(R) = Rf + β × (Rm - Rf)
Risk Premium (Rm - Rf) — extra return demanded for bearing market risk
Q2: An investment costs $50,000 and generates $12,000/year for 5 years. Cost of capital is 8%. Should you accept?
  • A) Yes, payback is 4.17 years
  • B) No, NPV is negative
  • C) Yes, NPV ≈ $47,888 - $50,000 = -$2,112 → Reject
  • D) Cannot determine without IRR
Show Answer
Answer: C
PVA = $12,000 × [1 - (1.08)⁻⁵] / 0.08 = $12,000 × 3.9927 = $47,912. NPV = $47,912 - $50,000 = -$2,088 ≈ Reject.
Trick: Don't just look at payback. Always use NPV for decision.

BTW1042 — Business Law

Elements of a Valid Contract Essential — Essay Q
1. Offer Clear, definite promise. Invitation to treat ≠ offer (e.g., advertisements, displays).
2. Acceptance Unqualified agreement to terms. Must be communicated. Silence ≠ acceptance (Felthouse v Bindley).
3. Consideration Something of value exchanged. Must be sufficient (not necessarily adequate). Past consideration is not valid.
4. Intention Social/domestic agreements — presumed no intention (Balfour v Balfour). Commercial — presumed intention exists.
5. Capacity Minors, mentally incapacitated, intoxicated persons may lack capacity.
6. Legality Purpose must be lawful. Illegal contracts are void.

Q3: Which case established the principle of separate legal personality?
  • A) Donoghue v Stevenson
  • B) Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd
  • C) Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co
  • D) Balfour v Balfour
Show Answer
Answer: B
Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd [1897] — Mr Salomon incorporated his business. When it failed, creditors tried to make him personally liable. House of Lords held company is separate legal person from its shareholders.

ECM1953 — Economics

Elasticity Formulas Calculations
Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)
PED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / (% Change in Price)
Midpoint Formula (more accurate)
PED = [(Q₂ - Q₁) / ((Q₁ + Q₂)/2)] / [(P₂ - P₁) / ((P₁ + P₂)/2)]
PED > 1 Elastic — quantity responsive to price change. Luxury goods.
PED < 1 Inelastic — quantity unresponsive. Necessities, addictions.
PED = 1 Unit elastic — % change in Q = % change in P
PED = 0 Perfectly inelastic — vertical demand curve
⚠ Exam Trap

Total Revenue test: If demand is elastic, price increase → revenue decreases. If inelastic, price increase → revenue increases.

Hypothesis Testing Steps 5-Step Process
  1. State H₀ and H₁ — H₀ always has equality (=, ≤, ≥)
  2. Choose α — usually 0.05 (5%)
  3. Calculate test statistic — Z or t
  4. Find critical value / p-value
  5. Decision: Reject H₀ if p < α OR test stat > critical value
Z-test for Mean
Z = (x̄ - μ₀) / (σ/√n)
t-test for Mean
t = (x̄ - μ₀) / (s/√n)
Regression Predictive Modelling
Simple Linear Regression
ŷ = b₀ + b₁x
Slope (b₁)
b₁ = Σ(x - x̄)(y - ȳ) / Σ(x - x̄)² = Cov(x,y) / Var(x)
R-squared
R² = SSR / SST = 1 - (SSE / SST)
Interpretation of b₁
For every 1-unit increase in x, y changes by b₁ units.

Percentage of variation in y explained by x. R² = 0.75 means 75% of y's variation is explained by the model.

Q4: A division has contribution margin of $50,000 and allocated fixed costs of $30,000. If the division is dropped, $20,000 of fixed costs are avoidable. Should the division be dropped?
  • A) Yes, saves $30,000
  • B) No, lose $50,000 CM but only save $20,000 → Net loss $30,000
  • C) Yes, profit increases by $10,000
  • D) Indifferent
Show Answer
Answer: B
Lost CM: $50,000. Avoidable FC saved: $20,000. Net effect: -$50,000 + $20,000 = -$30,000 (profit decreases). The $10,000 unavoidable fixed cost continues regardless — irrelevant to decision.

Probability & Distributions Foundations
Conditional Probability
P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)
Bayes' Theorem
P(A|B) = P(B|A) · P(A) / P(B)
Z-Score (Standard Normal)
Z = (X - μ) / σ
Binomial n trials, p success. Mean = np, Var = np(1-p)
Poisson Rare events. Mean = λ, Var = λ
Normal Symmetrical, bell-shaped. ~68% within ±1σ, ~95% within ±2σ
t-Distribution Used when σ unknown, small n. Heavier tails than normal.
Sampling & Central Limit Theorem Must Know
Standard Error of Mean
SE = σ / √n
Confidence Interval (Mean, σ unknown)
x̄ ± t(α/2, n-1) × (s / √n)
Confidence Interval (Proportion)
p̂ ± Z(α/2) × √(p̂(1-p̂)/n)
Central Limit Theorem
For n ≥ 30, the sampling distribution of x̄ is approximately normal regardless of population distribution.
⚠ Exam Trap

Always use n-1 for sample variance. Use Z when σ is known or n is large; use t when σ is unknown and n is small.

Hypothesis Testing 5-Step Framework
  1. State H₀ and H₁ — H₀ always has equality
  2. Choose α — usually 0.05
  3. Calculate test statistic
  4. Find critical value or p-value
  5. Decision: Reject H₀ if p < α or |test stat| > critical value
t-Test for Mean
t = (x̄ - μ₀) / (s/√n)
Two-Sample t-Test
t = (x̄₁ - x̄₂) / √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂)
Error TypeDescriptionProbability
Type IReject H₀ when it's true (false positive)α
Type IIFail to reject H₀ when it's false (false negative)β
Simple Linear Regression OLS Estimation
Model
y = β₀ + β₁x + ε
Slope Estimate
β̂₁ = Cov(x,y) / Var(x) = Σ(x-x̄)(y-ȳ) / Σ(x-x̄)²
Intercept Estimate
β̂₀ = ȳ - β̂₁x̄
R² (Coefficient of Determination)
R² = 1 - (SS_res / SS_tot) = Proportion of variance explained
Interpretation of β̂₁
For every 1-unit increase in x, y changes by β̂₁ units.
⚠ Exam Trap

High R² does NOT imply causation. Correlation ≠ Causation. Always consider omitted variable bias and research design.

Multiple Regression & Inference Econometric
Model
Y = β₀ + β₁X₁ + β₂X₂ + ... + βₖXₖ + u
t-Statistic (Individual Significance)
t = β̂ⱼ / SE(β̂ⱼ) ~ t(n-k-1)
F-Statistic (Overall Significance)
F = (R²/k) / ((1-R²)/(n-k-1))
Ceteris Paribus
Each coefficient βⱼ represents the change in Y for a 1-unit change in Xⱼ, holding all other variables constant.
⚠ Exam Trap

Use F-test for joint hypotheses (testing multiple coefficients = 0 simultaneously). Do NOT use multiple t-tests — inflates Type I error (family-wise error).

Q5: In a multiple regression, the coefficient on Price is -2.5 with a standard error of 0.8. What is the t-statistic and is it significant at 5%?
  • A) t = -2.0; Not significant
  • B) t = -3.125; Significant (|t| > ~1.96)
  • C) t = -2.5; Marginally significant
  • D) Cannot determine without sample size
Show Answer
Answer: B
t = -2.5 / 0.8 = -3.125. For large samples, critical value ≈ 1.96. |−3.125| > 1.96 → Significant at 5%. We reject H₀: β_price = 0. Each $1 price increase is associated with a 2.5 unit decrease in quantity demanded, holding other factors constant.
Q6: A state introduces a minimum wage policy. Employment was 100 before and 115 after in the treated state, and 100 → 105 in the control state. What is the DiD estimate?
  • A) 15
  • B) 5
  • C) 10
  • D) Cannot determine
Show Answer
Answer: C
Treated change = 115 - 100 = +15. Control change = 105 - 100 = +5. DiD = 15 - 5 = 10. The policy increased employment by 10 units net of trends. Key assumption: parallel trends (both states would have followed same trend absent treatment).
Q7: You run an A/B test with 1,000 users in each group. Group A converts at 12%, Group B at 15%. Is this significant at 5%?
  • A) Yes, Z ≈ 1.96 → significant
  • B) No, need larger sample
  • C) Yes, but only at 10%
  • D) Cannot tell without standard deviation
Show Answer
Answer: A
Pooled p̂ = (120+150)/2000 = 0.135. SE = √(0.135×0.865×(1/1000+1/1000)) = 0.0153. Z = (0.15-0.12)/0.0153 ≈ 1.96. At 5% two-tailed, critical Z = 1.96. Result is just significant.
Regression Output Checklist Exam Strategy
  1. ✅ Check sign and magnitude of coefficients — do they make economic sense?
  2. ✅ Check statistical significance — p-value < 0.05?
  3. ✅ Check economic significance — is the effect size practically important?
  4. ✅ Check overall model fit — R², F-test significant?
  5. ✅ Check sample size — is it large enough for reliable inference?
  6. ✅ Consider CLRM assumptions — any violations?
  7. ✅ Consider causality — is the design causal or just correlational?
  8. ✅ Use adjusted R² when comparing models with different numbers of predictors
