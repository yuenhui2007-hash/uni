Q1: A company receives $12,000 cash for services to be performed next month. What is the correct entry?
  • A) Dr Cash $12,000; Cr Revenue $12,000
  • B) Dr Cash $12,000; Cr Unearned Revenue $12,000
  • C) Dr Revenue $12,000; Cr Cash $12,000
  • D) Dr Prepaid Revenue $12,000; Cr Cash $12,000
Show Answer
Answer: B
Cash received before earning = liability (Unearned Revenue). Revenue recognition principle — recognise when earned, not when cash received.

BFW1001 — Foundations of Finance

Time Value of Money Calculators Allowed
Future Value (Lump Sum)
FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ
Present Value (Lump Sum)
PV = FV / (1 + r)ⁿ
Future Value of Annuity
FVA = PMT × [(1 + r)ⁿ - 1] / r
Present Value of Annuity
PVA = PMT × [1 - (1 + r)⁻ⁿ] / r
⚠ Exam Trap

Always check if payments are at beginning (annuity due) or end (ordinary annuity) of period. Annuity due = multiply ordinary annuity result by (1+r).

NPV & IRR Investment Appraisal
Net Present Value
NPV = Σ [CFₜ / (1 + r)ᵗ] - Initial Investment
Decision Rule
NPV > 0 → Accept; NPV < 0 → Reject
IRR
Discount rate where NPV = 0. If IRR > required return → Accept.
Risk & Return Conceptual
Systematic Risk Market-wide, non-diversifiable. Measured by beta (β).
Unsystematic Risk Firm-specific, diversifiable. Reduced via portfolio diversification.
Required Return (CAPM) E(R) = Rf + β × (Rm - Rf)
Risk Premium (Rm - Rf) — extra return demanded for bearing market risk
Q2: An investment costs $50,000 and generates $12,000/year for 5 years. Cost of capital is 8%. Should you accept?
  • A) Yes, payback is 4.17 years
  • B) No, NPV is negative
  • C) Yes, NPV ≈ $47,888 - $50,000 = -$2,112 → Reject
  • D) Cannot determine without IRR
Show Answer
Answer: C
PVA = $12,000 × [1 - (1.08)⁻⁵] / 0.08 = $12,000 × 3.9927 = $47,912. NPV = $47,912 - $50,000 = -$2,088 ≈ Reject.
Trick: Don't just look at payback. Always use NPV for decision.

BTW1042 — Business Law

Elements of a Valid Contract Essential — Essay Q
1. Offer Clear, definite promise. Invitation to treat ≠ offer (e.g., advertisements, displays).
2. Acceptance Unqualified agreement to terms. Must be communicated. Silence ≠ acceptance (Felthouse v Bindley).
3. Consideration Something of value exchanged. Must be sufficient (not necessarily adequate). Past consideration is not valid.
4. Intention Social/domestic agreements — presumed no intention (Balfour v Balfour). Commercial — presumed intention exists.
5. Capacity Minors, mentally incapacitated, intoxicated persons may lack capacity.
6. Legality Purpose must be lawful. Illegal contracts are void.

Q3: Which case established the principle of separate legal personality?
  • A) Donoghue v Stevenson
  • B) Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd
  • C) Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Co
  • D) Balfour v Balfour
Show Answer
Answer: B
Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd [1897] — Mr Salomon incorporated his business. When it failed, creditors tried to make him personally liable. House of Lords held company is separate legal person from its shareholders.

ECM1953 — Economics

Elasticity Formulas Calculations
Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)
PED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / (% Change in Price)
Midpoint Formula (more accurate)
PED = [(Q₂ - Q₁) / ((Q₁ + Q₂)/2)] / [(P₂ - P₁) / ((P₁ + P₂)/2)]
PED > 1 Elastic — quantity responsive to price change. Luxury goods.
PED < 1 Inelastic — quantity unresponsive. Necessities, addictions.
PED = 1 Unit elastic — % change in Q = % change in P
PED = 0 Perfectly inelastic — vertical demand curve
⚠ Exam Trap

Total Revenue test: If demand is elastic, price increase → revenue decreases. If inelastic, price increase → revenue increases.

ETW1001 — Business Statistics

Descriptive Stats Formulas
Mean
x̄ = Σx / n
Sample Variance
s² = Σ(x - x̄)² / (n - 1)
Standard Deviation
s = √s²
Coefficient of Variation
CV = (s / x̄) × 100%
⚠ Exam Trap

Sample variance uses n-1 (degrees of freedom). Population variance uses n. In exams, unless stated "population", assume sample.

Confidence Intervals Interval Estimation
CI for Mean (σ known)
x̄ ± Z(α/2) × (σ/√n)
CI for Mean (σ unknown)
x̄ ± t(α/2, n-1) × (s/√n)
CI for Proportion
p̂ ± Z(α/2) × √(p̂(1-p̂)/n)
Z critical values 90%: 1.645 | 95%: 1.96 | 99%: 2.576
Hypothesis Testing Steps 5-Step Process
  1. State H₀ and H₁ — H₀ always has equality (=, ≤, ≥)
  2. Choose α — usually 0.05 (5%)
  3. Calculate test statistic — Z or t
  4. Find critical value / p-value
  5. Decision: Reject H₀ if p < α OR test stat > critical value
Z-test for Mean
Z = (x̄ - μ₀) / (σ/√n)
t-test for Mean
t = (x̄ - μ₀) / (s/√n)
Regression Predictive Modelling
Simple Linear Regression
ŷ = b₀ + b₁x
Slope (b₁)
b₁ = Σ(x - x̄)(y - ȳ) / Σ(x - x̄)² = Cov(x,y) / Var(x)
R-squared
R² = SSR / SST = 1 - (SSE / SST)
Interpretation of b₁
For every 1-unit increase in x, y changes by b₁ units.
Interpretation of R²
Percentage of variation in y explained by x. R² = 0.75 means 75% of y's variation is explained by the model.

ACW2220 — Management Accounting

Cost Classifications Foundation
By BehaviourBy TraceabilityBy Relevance
Fixed — constant totalDirect — traceable to cost objectRelevant — future, differs
Variable — constant per unitIndirect — allocated (overhead)Irrelevant — sunk, same
Semi-variable — mixedOpportunity cost — forgone benefit
Total Cost (Linear)
Y = a + bX
a = total fixed cost | b = variable cost per unit | X = activity level
Cost Estimation Methods High-Low & Regression
High-Low Method
Variable cost per unit (b) = (Cost at high - Cost at low) / (Activity high - Activity low)
Fixed cost (a) = Total cost - (b × Activity level)
⚠ Exam Trap

Use the highest and lowest activity levels, NOT highest and lowest costs. Outliers can distort results.

Overhead Allocation Traditional vs ABC
TraditionalActivity-Based Costing (ABC)
Single plant-wide rate or departmental ratesMultiple cost pools, multiple cost drivers
Based on volume (e.g., direct labour hours)Based on activities that cause costs
Distorts product costs when overheads not driven by volumeMore accurate but more expensive to implement
Predetermined Overhead Rate
POHR = Estimated Total Overhead / Estimated Total Allocation Base
Short-Term Decision Making Relevant Costing
Special Order Accept if price > relevant cost. Consider spare capacity.
Make-or-Buy Compare internal variable cost + avoidable fixed cost vs external purchase price.
Product Mix (Constraint) Maximise contribution margin per unit of scarce resource.
Add/Drop Segment Compare lost contribution margin vs avoidable fixed costs.
⚠ Exam Trap

Common fixed costs (allocated HQ costs) are NOT relevant — they don't change regardless of decision. Only include costs that actually change.

Variance Analysis Flexible Budget
Total Variance
Actual Cost - Standard Cost
Price Variance
(Actual Price - Standard Price) × Actual Quantity
Quantity/Efficiency Variance
(Actual Quantity - Standard Quantity) × Standard Price
Favourable vs Adverse
Favourable (F) = actual cost < standard cost (good). Adverse (A) = actual cost > standard cost (bad).
Q4: A division has contribution margin of $50,000 and allocated fixed costs of $30,000. If the division is dropped, $20,000 of fixed costs are avoidable. Should the division be dropped?
  • A) Yes, saves $30,000
  • B) No, lose $50,000 CM but only save $20,000 → Net loss $30,000
  • C) Yes, profit increases by $10,000
  • D) Indifferent
Show Answer
Answer: B
Lost CM: $50,000. Avoidable FC saved: $20,000. Net effect: -$50,000 + $20,000 = -$30,000 (profit decreases). The $10,000 unavoidable fixed cost continues regardless — irrelevant to decision.

Common Calculation Mistakes Avoid These
  • ❌ Using n instead of n-1 for sample variance
  • ❌ Forgetting to square root standard deviation
  • ❌ Mixing up beginning vs end of period for annuities
  • ❌ Using nominal rate instead of periodic rate (r/12 for monthly)
  • ❌ Not converting percentages to decimals (10% = 0.10, not 0.10%)
  • ❌ Including sunk costs in relevant cost analysis
  • ❌ Forgetting that drawings reduce equity, not expenses
Essay Question Structure Law / Management
  1. Introduction: Define key terms, state your position
  2. Body paragraphs: Point → Explain → Example → Link back to question
  3. Use cases: "In Salomon v Salomon, the court held that..."
  4. Critical analysis: "However, this principle has been criticised because..."
  5. Conclusion: Summarise, don't introduce new points
